# Abstract

BAP1 is a tumor-suppressor gene that removes the histone modification H2AK119ub, traditionally understood to mediate gene repression. Its loss causes severe neurodevelopmental defects including epilepsy, but how it shapes DNA methylation over the course of neurodevelopment has never been characterized. Given that H2AK119ub was found to modulate chromatin conformation and increase MeCP2 binding in euchromatin, we profiled simultaneous 5-methylcytosine and 5-hydroxymethylcytosine in mouse cerebellum. We found a pattern of reciprocal changes at 8 in 10 dysregulated gene body loci, where 5mC increased and 5hmC decreased in the BAP1 mutant. At active promoters, we observe preferential hypermethylation, whereas Polycomb domains are protected by heterochromatin and associated with hypomethylation. Additionally, where H2AK119ub increases, genes are 5 times more likely to be hypermethylated, and this modification is strongly predictive of transcriptional downregulation. 
Analysis of these differentially expressed genes revealed the greatest significance for neuronal and synaptic loci, where BAP1-loss redistributes MeCP2 binding. We observe that MeCP2 increase over the course of neurodevelopment is doubled in our mutant, and strongly correlates with enriched H2AK119ub. As total methylation remains constant despite this shift towards repressive chromatin states, our model is consistent with impaired 5hmC conversion by TET enzymes and greater 5mC retention. These findings indicate that BAP1 is required for normal regulation of chromatin state, and its loss leads to a global redistribution of DNA methylation. The elevation of H2AK119ub results in MeCP2 selectively responding to chromatin changes, thus dysregulating neuronal and synaptic gene expression during cerebellar neurodevelopment.
